=== PROBLEM FORMULATION ===

Groups and objectives.
Let X ⊆ R^d be a feature space and Y a label
space, and let the population be partitioned into N groups indexed by
i ∈ N (demographic categories, devices, environments) with
prevalences π ∈ ΔN, π_i=P[group i]. Group i
carries a distribution D_i on XtimesY and
the risk

 
f_i(θ) = E_(X,Y)simD_i[ℓ(m(X;θ),Y)],
 θinC ⊆ R^d',

where m(· ;θ) is the predictor and ℓ the loss. Empirical risk
minimization weights the groups by prevalence, F_π= Σ _iπ_i f_i, and a
majority group therefore dominates the trained model. The remedy is to fix an
importance distribution p ∈ ΔN and minimize

 
F_p(θ) = Σ _i=1^Np_i f_i(θ),
 
θ^* ∈ argmin_θinCF_p(θ).

Uniform p is the category-uniform objective, p_i ∝ 1/π_i the
inverse-prevalence one, and p may equally encode a fairness constraint or
be imposed externally . Throughout,

Assumption. 

C is convex and compact with diameter D; each f_i is convex on
C and 0<= f_i<= M; and every stochastic subgradient
g_i(θ) of f_i returned by the sampler satisfies
E\|g_i(θ)\|^2<= G^2 on C.

 These are the assumptions of 
restated for groups; they place no restriction on p or on π.

Prevalence versus importance.
Minibatch SGD does not see p. At step t the sampler draws a batch B^t of
b examples; write

 
A^t = \ i ∈ N: B^t contains an example from group i \

for the active set of groups, let \A_j\_j ∈ M enumerate its
support, and let q_j=P[A^t=A_j] be the composition law.
The observation rate of group i is r_i=P[i ∈ A^t]= Σ _j: i ∈ A_jq_j;
under i.i.d. sampling r_i=1-(1-π_i)^b, so a group whose prevalence is
below the resolution of the batch is absent from most batches whatever p
says. This is federated learning under partial participation: A^t is the set
of participating clients, q the availability distribution, p the
importance distribution . We treat
q as known; otherwise it is estimated offline by simulating the sampler, as
in our experiments.

What group-blind SGD minimizes.
The group-blind update averages the per-example subgradients of B^t, which
assigns group i ∈ A^t the weight π_widehat^t_i, its share of the
batch, and assigns absent groups zero. Averaging first over the batch given
A^t and then over A^t ~ q is the hierarchical experiment of
, and it names the function SGD is in fact
minimizing.

Proposition (Surrogate objective). 

Under the assumption, group-blind minibatch SGD is a stochastic
subgradient method on F_p_tilde with

 
p_tilde_i = Σ _j: i ∈ A_j q_j E[π_widehat^t_i | A^t=A_j],

a probability vector. For i.i.d. sampling p_tilde=π; for uniform
weighting within the batch p_tilde_i= Σ _j: i ∈ A_jq_j/|A_j|, the
surrogate of agnostic FedAvg .
Consequently, for every θinC,

 
|F_p(θ)-F_p_tilde(θ)| <= M\|p-p\_tilde|_1 ,

and any method that minimizes F_p_tilde exactly incurs the excess
F_p(θ^*_p_tilde)-F_p(θ^*)<= 2M\|p-p\_tilde|_1 on
the equation, which does not vanish with T.

Proof. 
Conditionally on A^t=A_j the within-group averages are unbiased for
∂ f_i, so
E[batch subgradient | A^t=A_j]= Σ _i ∈ A_jE[π_widehat^t_i | A_j] ∂ f_i(θ);
averaging over q gives Σ _ip_tilde_i ∂ f_i=∂ F_p_tilde,
and Σ _ip_tilde_i= Σ _j q_j Σ _i ∈ A_jE[π_widehat^t_i | A_j]=1.
Both bounds follow from 0<= f_i<= M and the two-point inequality
F_p(θ^*_p_tilde)-F_p(θ^*)<=
[F_p-F_p_tilde](θ^*_p_tilde)+[F_p_tilde-F_p](θ^*).

the proposition says that the choice of p in
the equation is decorative unless the aggregation step is changed: the
iterates converge, at the usual rate, to the minimizer of a function whose
weights are induced by the sampler rather than chosen, and the bias
p-p_tilde survives every stepsize schedule. Rebalancing the sampler is
only a partial fix: p_tilde_i<= r_i always, so a group with p_i>r_i
cannot be sampled into enough batches however the sampler is tilted, while
upscaling the examples that do appear reintroduces an amplitude distortion
that destabilizes the iterates . The question is the
one posed for federated aggregation in , read on
batches:

The next section answers it, and characterizes exactly when the answer is yes.

# SECTION 3: PROPOSED APPROACH
######################################################################

=== PROPOSED APPROACH: ALIGNMENT BY MASKED OPTIMAL TRANSPORT ===

Weights as a coupling.
Let Y[i,j]>= 0 be the weight assigned to group i when the active set is
A_j, with Y[i,j]=0 for i ∉ A_j, and let T[i,j]= q_jY[i,j]
be the joint mass carried from composition j to group i. Requiring that
the weights be a probability vector on each realized composition, and that
each group receive in expectation the mass p assigns it, is the
masked optimal transport (MOT) feasibility problem of
,

 
T1_M=p, 1_N^ᵀ T=q, T>= 0,
 supp(T) ⊆ E,

with mask E=\(i,j): i ∈ A_j\ encoding that a group absent from the batch
cannot be weighted. Unlike classical transport
 there is no cost to minimize: any coupling
respecting the equation serves, and the equation is the Kantorovich
problem with the indicator cost C[i,j]=∞ off E. the figure
is the picture.

When alignment is possible.
Existence of a coupling is a supply-demand question on the bipartite graph
E, and is settled by flow duality.

Theorem (Feasibility, ). 

For I ⊆ N let N(I)=\j:∃ i ∈ I, (i,j) ∈ E\
and S(I)=\j: A_j ⊆ I\. Problem the equation is feasible
if and only if

 
 Σ _jinS(I)q_j <= Σ _i ∈ Ip_i <= Σ _jinN(I)q_j
 ∀ I ⊆ N.

Proofs are in the appendix. Read on singletons, the equation becomes a
statement one can act on before training starts.

Corollary (Coverage law and severity). 

Feasibility of the equation requires P[A^t=\i\]<= p_i<= r_i for every
i, with r_i=P[i ∈ A^t] the observation rate of the section.
Writing

 
ν = Σ _i : p_i>r_i p_i

for the infeasible mass, ν=0 iff the upper constraints hold.
Under i.i.d. sampling of b examples the upper constraint reads
b>=log(1-p_i)/log(1-π_i); under K-of-N uniform group sampling it
reads p_i<= K/N.

Severity.
Importance may not exceed observation rate: a rare group is reachable only
if the batch is large enough to contain it often enough. The infeasible mass
ν is the axis we sweep: at ν=0 exact alignment is available, and as
ν grows more of p sits outside the set of row marginals the mask can
reach. A feasible and an infeasible regime are two points on
this continuum, and the section sweeps it instead.

Computing the plan.
Given feasibility, a coupling is obtained by iterative proportional fitting
(Sinkhorn scaling) on the masked support
: starting from any T^(0)>0 on
E with 1^ᵀ T^(0)=q, alternate

 
Tarrowdiag(p ⊘ T1) T, 
Tarrow T diag(q ⊘ T^ᵀ1)

until \|T1-p\|_1<=ε. Each sweep costs O(|E|) with
|E|<= NM and M the number of realized compositions rather than
2^N; the plan is computed once, offline, and the training loop adds one
lookup and one multiply per group per step. Under the equation the
iteration converges to a solution of the equation
, being block-coordinate ascent on the dual of
the entropic problem min\ Σ _E Tlog T: T1=p, 1^ᵀ T=q\.
Since 1_N^ᵀ T=q and Y=Tdiag(q)^-1, every column
Y[·,j] is a probability vector on A_j, the property each bound below
uses. the algorithm is the resulting method; γ=1 is the
risk-neutral rule analyzed here and γ<1 the risk-averse layer of
the section.

Analysis.
Everything rests on one identity, moved from
model averaging to gradient averaging. Let g_i(θ) be the stochastic
subgradient of f_i computed from B^tcap group i and
g_hat(θ)= Σ _i ∈ A^tY[i,j(t)] g_i(θ) the transport-weighted
batch subgradient of the algorithm. Transport removes bias; it is
not a variance reduction device.

Lemma (Unbiasedness). 

Let T satisfy the equation. Then E[g_hat(θ) | θ] ∈ ∂ F_p(θ)
for every θinC.

Proof. 
Conditionally on A^t=A_j, E[g_i(θ)] ∈ ∂ f_i(θ) for
i ∈ A_j. Averaging over A^t ~ q,
E[g_hat]= Σ _j q_j Σ _i ∈ A_jY[i,j] ∂ f_i(θ)
= Σ _i( Σ _j T[i,j])∂ f_i(θ)= Σ _i p_i ∂ f_i(θ),
the row-marginal constraint being used in the last step.

Lemma (Second moment, free of coverage). 

E\|g_hat(θ)\|^2<= G^2 for every θinC, with no
dependence on |A^t| or on N.

Proof. 
Y[·,j] is a probability vector on A_j, so
\| Σ _i Y[i,j]g_i\|^2<= Σ _i Y[i,j]\|g_i\|^2 by convexity of
\|·\|^2; take expectations and use Σ _j T[i,j]=p_i:
E\|g\_hat|^2<= Σ _i p_i E\|g_i\|^2<= G^2.

Theorem (Rate). 

Under the assumption, if the equation is feasible then
the algorithm with γ=1 and η=D/(GsqrtT) satisfies

 
E[F_p(θ_bar_T)]-F_p(θ^*) <= fracDGsqrtT .

The constant in the equation is that of full-participation SGD and is
independent of how many groups appear in a batch: a composition covering two
groups yields the same bound as one covering all N, though with larger
realized variance. The proof uses only the two lemmas and never refers to
|A^t|, which is why the rate of transfers verbatim
from aggregation of models to aggregation of gradients.

Outside the feasible region.
When the equation fails, the equation has no solution and the iteration
the equation does not diverge: its iterates accumulate at the coupling
whose row marginal p_hat= T1 is the KL-closest to p
over the reachable polytope
R(q,E)=\T1: T>= 0, 1^ᵀ T=q, supp(T) ⊆ E\
. The column marginal is met
exactly, so Lemmas and hold with p
replaced by p_hat: the algorithm is then unbiased SGD on the
surrogate F_p_hat, the closest objective the sampler can reach, and the
loss on the equation is a bias of exactly the shape of
the proposition. On singletons p_hat_i<= r_i, so the
groups with p_i>r_i are truncated at their observation rate and their
missing mass, ν up to renormalization, is redistributed.

Theorem (Infeasible regime). 

Let p_hat be the row marginal of the Sinkhorn limit,
θ^† ∈ argmin_CF_p_hat, and write
Φ(w)= F_p(argmin_C F_w) for the p-risk of
the minimizer of the w-weighted risk. Then

 
E[F_p(θ_bar_T)]-F_p(θ^*)
 = Φ(p_hat)-Φ(p)_alignment floor
 + ε_T(p_hat)_optimization residual ,

where ε_T(p_hat)=E F_p(θ_bar_T)-F_p(θ^†)
satisfies E F_p_hat(θ_bar_T)-F_p_hat(θ^†)<= DG/sqrtT
and ε_T(p_hat)-> 0 whenever θ_bar_T->θ^†.
Group-blind averaging obeys the equation with p_tilde in place of
p_hat. Writing Δ_floor=Φ(p_tilde)-Φ(p_hat)
for the alignment gain, transport improves on group-blind averaging
exactly when Δ_floor>ε_T(p_hat)-ε_T(p_tilde),
and every floor is bounded by Φ(w)-Φ(p)<= 2M\|p-w\|_1.

the theorem replaces the usual bias bound
 by an exact decomposition, both terms of which
are available before training: p_hat and p_tilde from (p,q,E), and
Φ exactly on instances with a closed-form or convex frontier. The gain is
fixed by the instance and decides the sign of the comparison; the residual is
paid by the estimator and, as the section shows, is a stepsize
artifact. The ℓ_1 bound is the weaker object and we report both, because
it turns out too loose to discriminate.

=== EXPERIMENTS ===

Instances.
Adult income classification : logistic regression
(d=109 with intercept, cross-entropy, η=0.1) on 100
race-homogeneous groups of 30 samples, the number of groups per race
proportional to its prevalence (White/Black/Asian-Pac./Amer-Ind./Other
=85/10/3/1/1); the importance target is the fairness target itself, 1/5
per race divided equally among its groups, so a group from the smallest
race carries p_i=0.2. IMDb-Wiki age regression
: linear regression of age on 128-d ResNet face
embeddings (η=10^-2) over the first 100 identities with 30
images, with the cubic importance target p_i ∝ (101-i)^3.
Observation rates are swept through a one-parameter family: on Adult
r ∝ p^β, β ∈ \0,tfrac14,tfrac12,tfrac34,1\, from
uniform observation (prevalence regime, ν=.60: the five groups of the
three smallest races hold 60\% of the target against r_iapprox K/m)
to importance-aligned (ν=0); on IMDb-Wiki r_i ∝ i^β,
β ∈ \0,tfrac12,1,tfrac32,2,3\, from uniform observation
(ν=.31, nine groups) to the mirror image of p (ν=.88, 41
groups), plus the aligned r_i ∝ 101-i (ν=0). Eleven instances,
ν from 0 to .88.

Protocol.
Each step observes K=3 groups drawn without replacement with
probabilities ∝ r, which induces q over the binom1003
compositions; q is estimated from 10^6 draws of the sampler and the plan
from the equation on the membership mask, once per instance. Each
observed group takes H=5 local steps on its own data before the weighted
average, the standard practical deviation from the one-step analysis,
applied to every rule alike since all rules consume the same local updates
from the same draw A^t. Rules: GAVOT; group-blind averaging, the
uniform average over the observed groups (the proposition
with p_tilde_i=r_i/K); the fixed multiplier m/K, the rescaling
(m/K) p_i that presumes uniform observation; and full, every group
every step weighted by p, the reference Φ(p). Stepsize
η_t=η/(1+t/1000) unless stated, 4000 steps, 5 seeds; we report
F_p averaged over the last 500 steps, mean pm std over seeds. On both
instances argmin F_w is available to machine precision (closed form for
least squares, Newton for the logistic loss), so the floors
Φ(p),Φ(p_hat),Φ(p_tilde) of the theorem are
exact rather than estimated, and in every instance with ν=0 the
iteration the equation converges to 10^-8, certifying the remaining
inequalities of the equation.

E1: the headline cells.
the table reports the four instances a practitioner would
recognize. Transport wins on three: Adult under prevalence-proportional
availability, 0.2269 against 0.2479 (t=45), and both IMDb cells,
86.06 against 91.63 (t=21) and 84.28 against 86.37 (t=13). On
Adult with importance-aligned availability the two coincide at the oracle
value, which is what the proposition predicts when
p_tilde=p: there is nothing to correct and transport does not manufacture a
difference. The fixed multiplier diverges on both aligned instances and is far
off on the other two, so naive rescaling is no alternative to solving the equation.

E2: the crossover, and where the sign comes from.
the table and the figure are the paper's main
evidence. On Adult, whose five skews are omitted from the table, the measured gain tracks
Δ_floor within 4\% at three of five
(.0134 against .0129, .0050 against .0049, .0002 against .0003)
and exceeds it at the two most infeasible (.0210 against .0110, .0181
against .0144), group-blind averaging sitting further above its floor. On IMDb-Wiki the gain is
uniformly below the floor by an excess optimization residual that is close to
constant in β, mean 1.9 in MSE units, and the measured sign flips
precisely where Δ_floor crosses that value, between
β=1.5 (2.56, gain +0.58) and β=2.0 (1.62, gain -0.03).
This is the theorem read at finite T: the sign of the
comparison is a quantity one computes in advance.

E3: the ℓ_1 bound is too loose to be the criterion.
The distances \|p-p\_hat|_1 and \|p-p\_tilde|_1 are also computed on every
instance, and \|p-p\_hat|_1<\|p-p\_tilde|_1 holds in all eleven, including
the three where transport loses or ties. The ℓ_1 form of
the theorem therefore never predicts a loss and cannot serve as
the criterion; the exact floors can.

E4: the residual is a stepsize artifact.
The left panel of the figure runs the identical instances at
constant stepsize. There transport loses on all six IMDb skews, by as much as
7.9, even at ν=.31 where the floors are 6.99 apart. The transport
weights Y[·,j] concentrate mass on rarely observed groups, so although
the lemma bounds the second moment identically for both rules,
the variance about the mean is larger, and at constant stepsize the iterates sit
on a noise floor proportional to it. Under η_t=η/(1+t/1000) that floor
decays, the measured curves fall onto their dashed floors, and every sign
reversal attributable to the stepsize disappears. The residual is a property of
the schedule, not of the estimator.

E6: the decomposition in closed form.
The mirrored IMDb-Wiki instance (ν=.88) is where
the theorem can be read off exactly. The Sinkhorn limit
truncates the target at the observation-rate ceiling,
p_hat_iapproxmin(p_i,r_i) up to renormalization (maximum deviation
.023), with \|p-p\_hat|_1=1.58 of a possible 2. The frontier is least
squares, so Φ(p)=82.9, matching the measured full plateau 82.95, and
Φ(p_hat)=105.9: the alignment floor is 23.0 and the realized bias
equals its exact expression |(p-p_hat)^ᵀ L(θ^†)|=30.7, where
L collects the per-group risks, while the ℓ_1 form 2M\|p-p\_hat|_1
is conservative since M>=max_i L_i(θ^†)approx 454.
Group-blind averaging has the same floor to two decimals,
Φ(p_tilde)=105.9: both surrogates starve the same groups, so
Δ_floor vanishes and the entire measured gap between the
two rules at this endpoint of the table is residual, which is
why it is the one instance where transport still loses under decay.

E5: risk aversion, and where it does not help.
Sweeping (α,γ) on a 10 × 10 grid, the overall objective is
minimized on the γ=1 edge on every instance, which is
κ(1,γ)=1 made visible and confirms that risk aversion costs the
average. The one place the CVaR layer earns its keep is the worst race on Adult
under prevalence availability, where GAVOT+CVaR at (0.2,0.7) reaches
0.3624 against 0.3679 for GAVOT and 0.3994 for group-blind averaging.
Per race, transport improves four of five (Black .179 vs .200, Asian-Pac
.368 vs .399, Am-Ind .175 vs .189, Other .073 vs .119) and regresses
only the majority group (.339 vs .332), which is what reweighting toward a
category-uniform target is supposed to do.
